
#Solving Equations

An equation is solved by finding the value of the unknown variable that makes both sides equal.

The goal is always:

$$
\text{Variable}=\text{Answer}
$$

---

# Types of Equations

The main equation types covered are:

1. Linear Equations
2. Quadratic Equations
3. Simultaneous Equations

---

# 1. Linear Equations

A linear equation has a variable with a power of 1.

General form:

$$
ax+b=c
$$

The highest power of the variable is:

$$
x^1=x
$$

---

# Linear Equation Method

## Step 1

Remove brackets if they exist.

## Step 2

Collect like terms on the same side.

* Variables on one side
* Constants on the other side

## Step 3

Simplify both sides.

## Step 4

Divide by the coefficient of the variable.

---

# Example 1

Solve:

$$
3x+5=20
$$

---

## Step 1: Move constants away from the variable

Subtract 5 from both sides:

$$
3x+5-5=20-5
$$

$$
3x=15
$$

---

## Step 2: Divide by the coefficient of \(x\)

$$
\frac{3x}{3}=\frac{15}{3}
$$

$$
x=5
$$

---

# Example 2

Solve:

$$
5x-7=18
$$

---

## Step 1: Move constants

Add 7 to both sides:

$$
5x-7+7=18+7
$$

$$
5x=25
$$

---

## Step 2: Divide by coefficient

$$
\frac{5x}{5}=\frac{25}{5}
$$

$$
x=5
$$

---

# Linear Equations With Brackets

Example:

$$
3(x+4)=21
$$

---

## Step 1: Expand brackets

$$
3(x)+3(4)=21
$$

$$
3x+12=21
$$

---

## Step 2: Move constants

Subtract 12:

$$
3x+12-12=21-12
$$

$$
3x=9
$$

---

## Step 3: Divide

$$
x=3
$$

---

# Linear Equations With Variables On Both Sides

Example:

$$
5x+4=2x+19
$$

---

## Step 1: Move variables to one side

Subtract \(2x\):

$$
5x-2x+4=19
$$

$$
3x+4=19
$$

---

## Step 2: Move constants

Subtract 4:

$$
3x=15
$$

---

## Step 3: Divide

$$
x=5
$$

---

# 2. Quadratic Equations

A quadratic equation has the form:

$$
ax^2+bx+c=0
$$

The highest power of the variable is:

$$
x^2
$$

---

# Quadratic Equation Methods

Quadratics can be solved using:

1. Factorisation
2. Quadratic Formula

---

# Method 1: Solving Quadratics by Factorisation

## Steps

### Step 1

Move everything to one side.

$$
ax^2+bx+c=0
$$

### Step 2

Factorise the quadratic.

### Step 3

Use the zero product rule:

If:

$$
(a)(b)=0
$$

then:

$$
a=0
$$

or

$$
b=0
$$

---

# Example 1

Solve:

$$
x^2+5x+6=0
$$

---

## Step 1: Factorise

Find two numbers that:

Multiply to:

$$
6
$$

Add to:

$$
5
$$

Numbers:

$$
2 \text{ and } 3
$$

Therefore:

$$
(x+2)(x+3)=0
$$

---

## Step 2: Solve each bracket

First bracket:

$$
x+2=0
$$

$$
x=-2
$$

Second bracket:

$$
x+3=0
$$

$$
x=-3
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
x=-2 \text{ or } x=-3
$$

---

# Example 2: Quadratic Requiring Rearranging

Solve:

$$
x^2+3=5x
$$

---

## Step 1: Move everything to one side

$$
x^2-5x+3=0
$$

---

## Step 2: Factorise if possible

In this case, factorisation is difficult.

Use the quadratic formula.

---

# Method 2: Quadratic Formula

For:

$$
ax^2+bx+c=0
$$

The solution is:

$$
x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}
$$

# Quadratic Formula Method

## Step 1

Identify:

$$
a=\_\_
$$

$$
b=\_\_
$$

$$
c=\_\_
$$

---

## Step 2

Write the formula.

---

## Step 3

Substitute values carefully.

---

## Step 4

Calculate the discriminant:

$$
b^2-4ac
$$

---

## Step 5

Simplify both solutions.

---

# Example

Solve:

$$
x^2-5x+3=0
$$

---

## Step 1: Identify values

Compare:

$$
ax^2+bx+c=0
$$

Therefore:

$$
a=1
$$

$$
b=-5
$$

$$
c=3
$$

---

## Step 2: Substitute into formula

$$
x=\frac{-(-5)\pm\sqrt{(-5)^2-4(1)(3)}}{2(1)}
$$

---

## Step 3: Simplify

$$
x=\frac{5\pm\sqrt{25-12}}{2}
$$

$$
x=\frac{5\pm\sqrt{13}}{2}
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
x=\frac{5+\sqrt{13}}{2}
$$

or

$$
x=\frac{5-\sqrt{13}}{2}
$$

---

# Quadratic Formula Checklist

Before submitting:

✓ Equation is equal to zero
✓ Values of \(a,b,c\) are written
✓ Formula is written
✓ Substitution is shown
✓ Both answers are calculated

---

# Exam Rule

Never only write:

$$
x=5
$$

Even if the answer is correct, marks are usually awarded for the method.

Always show:

1. Rearranging
2. Formula or factorisation
3. Working
4. Final answers

---

This matches the same "show the method to collect marks" approach used in your multiplication and factorisation notes.
