

Factorisation of Algebraic Expressions

Factorisation is the process of writing an algebraic expression as a multiplication of smaller expressions called **factors**.

Factorisation is the opposite of expansion.

Example:

Expansion:

$$
3(x+4)=3x+12
$$

Factorisation:

$$
3x+12=3(x+4)
$$

---

# Factorisation Types

The main factorisation methods are:

1. Common Factor
2. Difference of Squares
3. Trinomial Factorisation
4. Factorisation by Grouping

---

# 1. Common Factor

A common factor is a number or variable that appears in every term.

## Method

### Step 1

Find the highest common factor (HCF).

### Step 2

Divide every term by the common factor.

### Step 3

Write the common factor outside the bracket.

---

## Example 1

Factorise:

$$
6x+12
$$

### Step 1: Identify the common factor

Family list:

1. Constants
2. Variables

Terms:

$$
6x+12
$$

Common factor:

$$
6
$$

---

### Step 2: Divide each term by 6

$$
\frac{6x}{6}+\frac{12}{6}
$$

$$
x+2
$$

---

### Step 3: Write the factorised form

$$
6(x+2)
$$

---

# Example 2

Factorise:

$$
8x^2+12x
$$

### Step 1: Find common factor

Numbers:

$$
8 \text{ and } 12
$$

HCF:

$$
4
$$

Variables:

$$
x^2 \text{ and } x
$$

Common variable:

$$
x
$$

Common factor:

$$
4x
$$

---

### Step 2: Divide every term

$$
\frac{8x^2}{4x}+\frac{12x}{4x}
$$

$$
2x+3
$$

---

### Step 3: Final answer

$$
4x(2x+3)
$$

---

# 2. Factorisation of Binomials

A binomial has two terms.

$$
a+b
$$

or

$$
a-b
$$

The main binomial factorisation method is **difference of squares**.

---

# Difference of Squares

Rule:

$$
a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)
$$

A difference of squares must have:

1. Two terms
2. A subtraction sign
3. Both terms must be perfect squares

---

## Example

Factorise:

$$
x^2-25
$$

### Step 1: Identify squares

$$
x^2=(x)^2
$$

$$
25=(5)^2
$$

---

### Step 2: Apply rule

$$
(x+5)(x-5)
$$

---

# Example 2

Factorise:

$$
9x^2-16
$$

### Step 1: Identify squares

$$
9x^2=(3x)^2
$$

$$
16=4^2
$$

---

### Step 2: Apply rule

$$
(3x+4)(3x-4)
$$

---

# 3. Factorisation of Trinomials

A trinomial has three terms.

General form:

$$
ax^2+bx+c
$$

The goal is to find two brackets.

$$
(ax+b)(cx+d)
$$

---

# Simple Trinomials

When the coefficient of \(x^2\) is 1:

$$
x^2+bx+c
$$

Find two numbers that:

1. Multiply to give \(c\)
2. Add to give \(b\)

---

## Example

Factorise:

$$
x^2+7x+12
$$

### Step 1: Identify numbers

Need two numbers that:

Multiply:

$$
12
$$

Add:

$$
7
$$

Numbers:

$$
3 \text{ and } 4
$$

---

### Step 2: Write brackets

$$
(x+3)(x+4)
$$

---

# Trinomials With Negative Signs

## Example

Factorise:

$$
x^2-5x+6
$$

Need two numbers that:

Multiply:

$$
6
$$

Add:

$$
-5
$$

Numbers:

$$
-2 \text{ and } -3
$$

Answer:

$$
(x-2)(x-3)
$$

---

# 4. Complex Trinomials

When the coefficient of \(x^2\) is not 1.

Example:

$$
2x^2+7x+3
$$

---

## Method: Split the Middle Term

### Step 1

Multiply:

$$
a \times c
$$

where:

$$
a=2
$$

$$
c=3
$$

$$
2\times3=6
$$

---

### Step 2

Find two numbers that:

Multiply to:

$$
6
$$

Add to:

$$
7
$$

Numbers:

$$
6 \text{ and } 1
$$

---

### Step 3

Split the middle term

$$
2x^2+6x+x+3
$$

---

### Step 4

Factorise by grouping

Group:

$$
(2x^2+6x)+(x+3)
$$

Common factors:

$$
2x(x+3)+1(x+3)
$$

---

### Step 5

Final factorisation

$$
(2x+1)(x+3)
$$

---

# 5. Factorisation By Grouping

Used when there are four terms.

Example:

$$
x^3+3x^2+2x+6
$$

---

## Step 1: Group terms

$$
(x^3+3x^2)+(2x+6)
$$

---

## Step 2: Find common factors

First group:

$$
x^2(x+3)
$$

Second group:

$$
2(x+3)
$$

---

## Step 3: Common bracket

$$
x^2(x+3)+2(x+3)
$$

Common factor:

$$
(x+3)
$$

---

Final answer:

$$
(x+3)(x^2+2)
$$

---

# Factorisation Checking Method

Always check your answer by expanding.

Example:

$$
(x+3)(x+4)
$$

Multiply:

$$
x(x)+x(4)+3(x)+3(4)
$$

$$
x^2+4x+3x+12
$$

Simplify:

$$
x^2+7x+12
$$

The answer matches the original expression.

---

# Factorisation Order Rule

Always check methods in this order:

1. Common factor
2. Difference of squares
3. Trinomial factorisation
4. Grouping

Always remove the common factor first before attempting another method.

Example:

$$
2x^2-18
$$

First remove common factor:

$$
2(x^2-9)
$$

Then difference of squares:

$$
2(x+3)(x-3)
$$

---

This follows the same teaching philosophy as your multiplication notes: **identify → apply method → show every term → simplify → verify.**
