# Solving Simultaneous Equations

Simultaneous equations are two equations that contain the same variables.

The solution is the pair of values that makes **both equations true**.

General form:

$$
ax+by=c
$$

$$
dx+ey=f
$$

The answer is written as:

$$
x=\_\_
$$

$$
y=\_\_
$$

---

# Method 1: Substitution

Substitution is used when one equation can easily be rearranged to make one variable the subject.

## Steps

### Step 1

Choose one equation and make one variable the subject.

### Step 2

Substitute that value into the other equation.

### Step 3

Solve for the remaining variable.

### Step 4

Substitute back to find the other variable.

---

# Example 1: Substitution

Solve:

$$
x+y=10
$$

$$
x-y=2
$$

---

## Step 1: Make one variable the subject

Use the first equation:

$$
x+y=10
$$

Make \(x\) the subject:

$$
x=10-y
$$

---

## Step 2: Substitute into the second equation

$$
x-y=2
$$

Replace \(x\):

$$
(10-y)-y=2
$$

---

## Step 3: Solve for \(y\)

Remove brackets:

$$
10-y-y=2
$$

Simplify:

$$
10-2y=2
$$

Move constants:

$$
-2y=2-10
$$

$$
-2y=-8
$$

Divide:

$$
y=4
$$

---

## Step 4: Substitute back

$$
x=10-y
$$

$$
x=10-4
$$

$$
x=6
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
x=6
$$

$$
y=4
$$

---

# Method 2: Elimination

Elimination removes one variable by adding or subtracting equations.

This method is useful when the variables have matching coefficients.

## Steps

### Step 1

Arrange equations vertically.

### Step 2

Multiply an equation if needed so one variable has matching coefficients.

### Step 3

Add or subtract equations to remove one variable.

### Step 4

Solve the remaining equation.

### Step 5

Substitute back to find the second variable.

---

# Example 2: Elimination

Solve:

$$
x+y=10
$$

$$
x-y=2
$$

---

## Step 1: Write equations underneath each other

$$
x+y=10
$$

$$
x-y=2
$$

---

## Step 2: Eliminate \(y\)

Add the equations:

$$
(x+y)+(x-y)=10+2
$$

---

## Step 3: Simplify

$$
2x=12
$$

Divide:

$$
x=6
$$

---

## Step 4: Substitute back

Use:

$$
x+y=10
$$

Replace \(x\):

$$
6+y=10
$$

Subtract 6:

$$
y=4
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
x=6
$$

$$
y=4
$$

---

# Elimination With Multiplication

Sometimes coefficients do not match.

Example:

$$
2x+y=7
$$

$$
3x+2y=12
$$

---

## Step 1: Make one variable match

Multiply the first equation by 2:

$$
2(2x+y=7)
$$

$$
4x+2y=14
$$

Now:

$$
4x+2y=14
$$

$$
3x+2y=12
$$

---

## Step 2: Eliminate \(y\)

Subtract equations:

$$
(4x+2y)-(3x+2y)=14-12
$$

---

## Step 3: Simplify

$$
x=2
$$

---

## Step 4: Substitute back

Use:

$$
2x+y=7
$$

$$
2(2)+y=7
$$

$$
4+y=7
$$

$$
y=3
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
x=2
$$

$$
y=3
$$

---

# Checking Your Answer

Always check by substituting your answers into both original equations.

Example:

Given:

$$
x=2
$$

$$
y=3
$$

First equation:

$$
2(2)+3=7
$$

$$
7=7
$$

Correct.

Second equation:

$$
3(2)+2(3)=12
$$

$$
12=12
$$

Correct.

---

# Exam Strategy

When choosing a method:

### Use Substitution when:

✓ One variable already has a coefficient of 1
✓ An equation is easy to rearrange

Example:

$$
x+y=8
$$

---

### Use Elimination when:

✓ Variables have matching coefficients
✓ Adding or subtracting removes a variable quickly

Example:

$$
2x+y=5
$$

$$
2x-y=3
$$

---

# Common Mistakes

## 1. Forgetting to change signs

Example:

$$
-(x+3)
$$

becomes:

$$
-x-3
$$

---

## 2. Not substituting into the original equation

Students often find one variable and stop.

Always find:

$$
x=\_\_
$$

and

$$
y=\_\_
$$

---

## 3. Not checking answers

A correct-looking answer can still contain calculation mistakes.

---
