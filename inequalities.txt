# Solving Inequalities

An inequality compares two values that are **not necessarily equal**.

Instead of using:

$$
=
$$

we use inequality symbols:

$$
<,\ >,\ \leq,\ \geq
$$

---

# Inequality Symbols

| Symbol   | Meaning                  |
| -------- | ------------------------ |
| \(<\)    | Less than                |
| \(>\)    | Greater than             |
| \(\leq\) | Less than or equal to    |
| \(\geq\) | Greater than or equal to |

Examples:

$$
x<5
$$

means:

$$
x \text{ is smaller than } 5
$$

$$
x\geq3
$$

means:

$$
x \text{ is 3 or greater}
$$

---

# Solving Linear Inequalities

Linear inequalities are solved using the same steps as linear equations.

The main difference:

**When multiplying or dividing by a negative number, the inequality sign must change direction.**

---

# Linear Inequality Method

## Step 1

Remove brackets if needed.

## Step 2

Collect variables on one side.

## Step 3

Collect constants on the other side.

## Step 4

Divide by the coefficient of the variable.

## Step 5

Check if division was by a negative number.

If yes, reverse the inequality sign.

---

# Example 1

Solve:

$$
x+5>12
$$

---

## Step 1: Move the constant

Subtract 5 from both sides:

$$
x+5-5>12-5
$$

$$
x>7
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
x>7
$$

---

# Example 2

Solve:

$$
3x-4\leq11
$$

---

## Step 1: Move constants

Add 4:

$$
3x-4+4\leq11+4
$$

$$
3x\leq15
$$

---

## Step 2: Divide by coefficient

$$
\frac{3x}{3}\leq\frac{15}{3}
$$

$$
x\leq5
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
x\leq5
$$

---

# Negative Division Rule

When dividing or multiplying by a negative number:

$$
\text{Reverse the inequality sign}
$$

Examples:

$$
5>-2
$$

becomes:

$$
-5<2
$$

The direction changes.

---

# Example 3: Negative Coefficient

Solve:

$$
-2x>8
$$

---

## Step 1: Divide by -2

Because we divide by a negative number, reverse the sign.

$$
\frac{-2x}{-2}<\frac{8}{-2}
$$

---

## Step 2: Simplify

$$
x<-4
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
x<-4
$$

---

# Inequalities With Brackets

Example:

$$
2(x+3)>14
$$

---

## Step 1: Expand brackets

$$
2(x)+2(3)>14
$$

$$
2x+6>14
$$

---

## Step 2: Move constants

Subtract 6:

$$
2x>8
$$

---

## Step 3: Divide

$$
x>4
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
x>4
$$

---

# Double Inequalities

A double inequality shows a range of values.

Example:

$$
2<x<7
$$

This means:

* \(x\) is greater than 2
* \(x\) is less than 7

The values are between 2 and 7.

---

# Solving Double Inequalities

Perform the same operation on all three parts.

---

# Example

Solve:

$$
3<2x+1<9
$$

---

## Step 1: Remove the constant

Subtract 1 from all parts:

$$
3-1<2x+1-1<9-1
$$

$$
2<2x<8
$$

---

## Step 2: Divide all parts by 2

$$
\frac{2}{2}<\frac{2x}{2}<\frac{8}{2}
$$

---

## Final Answer

$$
1<x<4
$$

---

# Number Line Representation

Inequalities can also be shown on a number line.

Rules:

### Open circle

Used for:

$$
<
$$

or

$$
>
$$

The value is **not included**.

Example:

$$
x>3
$$

---

### Closed circle

Used for:

$$
\leq
$$

or

$$
\geq
$$

The value **is included**.

Example:

$$
x\geq3
$$

---

# Inequality Checking Method

Choose a value that satisfies the answer and substitute it back.

Example:

Answer:

$$
x>4
$$

Choose:

$$
x=5
$$

Check:

$$
5>4
$$

Correct.

---

# Common Exam Mistakes

## 1. Forgetting to reverse the sign

Wrong:

$$
-2x>8
$$

$$
x>-4
$$

Correct:

$$
x<-4
$$

---

## 2. Treating inequalities exactly like equations

An equation has one answer:

$$
x=5
$$

An inequality has a range:

$$
x>5
$$

---

## 3. Forgetting the equal sign

Remember:

$$
\leq
$$

and

$$
\geq
$$

include the boundary value.

Example:

$$
x\leq5
$$

includes:

$$
x=5
$$
